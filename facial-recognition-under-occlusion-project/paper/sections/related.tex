\section{Related work}

Occluded and masked face recognition has its own surveys and benchmarks~\cite{zeng2021survey,deng2021mfr}; this paper uses only synthetic occluders and does not compare with methods built for occluded faces. The pretrained recognizers in the original project are VGG-Face~\cite{parkhi2015deep}, FaceNet~\cite{schroff2015facenet} (and DeepFace's 512-dimensional FaceNet), OpenFace~\cite{amos2016openface}, ArcFace~\cite{deng2019arcface}, and SFace~\cite{zhong2021sface}, as wrapped by DeepFace~\cite{serengil2020lightface}. The identification study uses SFace and an ArcFace ResNet-50 trained on WebFace600K from InsightFace~\cite{insightface}, and trains its own network with the large-margin cosine loss of CosFace~\cite{wang2018cosface} or a batch-hard triplet loss~\cite{hermans2017defense}. Its occlusion augmentation is a centered form of Cutout~\cite{devries2017cutout}, which masks a square at a random position, and is close to Random Erasing~\cite{zhong2020random}.

The project's attention layer is a spatial attention map in the spirit of CBAM's spatial module~\cite{woo2018cbam}: a single map, passed through a sigmoid, that scales every channel at each position. CBAM computes its map with a 7$\times$7 convolution of channel-wise average- and max-pooled features; the project's layer instead applies a 7$\times$7 convolution to all 64 channels of the first block's output.
