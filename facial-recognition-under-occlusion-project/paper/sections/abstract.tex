How well does face identification hold up when part of the face is covered, and do a spatial attention layer and training on occluded faces help a small convolutional neural network (CNN)? The original course project measured something narrower: each probe was a gallery image with a centered black square drawn on it, so the task was matching an occluded image to its own clean copy. Nearest neighbor on raw pixels, with no learning, matches \N{px_10_lo} to 100\% of those images with 10\% of the frame covered. For SFace, an approximate re-creation of the pipeline traces most of the collapse at 5 to 10\% of the frame to the face detector, which stops finding the face once the square appears; the other five pretrained models were not rerun. A new study tests identification on one identity-disjoint split of CelebA-HQ: \N{n_test_identities} test identities never seen in the CNNs' training, \N{n_probes} probes, faces aligned on the clean image before any occluder is drawn (likely an upper bound for a real pipeline), \N{n_conditions} conditions (clean and \N{n_occluded} synthetic occluders), two pretrained references, and a 2$\times$2 ablation of attention and occlusion augmentation with \N{n_seeds} seeds each. On aligned crops, ArcFace R50 identifies \N{r1_arc_s00}\% of probes clean and \N{r1_arc_s20}\% with 20\% of the crop covered. In the small CNN, occlusion augmentation adds \N{P1_abs} points of rank-1 on the centered squares it was trained with and \N{P2_abs} on average on occluders it never saw ($p<\N{P1_p}$, Holm $p<\N{P1_holm}$ for each), unevenly (from \N{aug_eye} on an eye band to \N{aug_low} on a lower-face band), and costs \N{aug_s00_abs} points on clean faces. The attention contrast, \N{P3} point, comes from noise fills and the lower-face band; on black squares, no main effect was detected, and attention costs \N{att_s00_abs} points on clean faces. These CNNs are weak identifiers (\N{cos_clean_lo} to \N{cos_clean_hi}\% clean rank-1), so the study is about the differences between them.
